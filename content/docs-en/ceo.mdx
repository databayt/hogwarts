## Why it pays

A small team loses most of its time to unclear priorities. The office turns every week into one or two tasks each that move a school toward paying, and checks them twice. Its whole job is the road on [Monetize](/docs/monetize): the first payment on 19 December, and break-even at about 320 paying students.

## What the office tracks

- Every product, every school, every deal in the CRM.
- Our money: price, costs, runway, and the road to break-even.
- Every team message in «المدرسة 2.0», and who promised what.
- The best of business thinking: strategy, pricing, sales and leadership.

## The week

| When     | The office                                                           |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sunday   | Writes the plan: one or two tasks each, with a date                  |
| Tuesday  | Asks each person for one line: done, doing, or stuck                 |
| Thursday | Reviews: done, not done, money, next week — and settles one argument |
| Any day  | Answers questions, drafts offers, contracts and messages             |

Short, regular check-ins catch a slip in days. A plan nobody reads until the end of the month catches it too late.

## How decisions move

- A one-page brief: what changed, what is stuck, what to decide.
- Every decision becomes tasks, messages and documents the same day.
- Nothing waits for a meeting.
- A decision that is easy to undo is made the same day by whoever owns the work. Only hard-to-undo ones (money, prices, contracts, people) wait for a yes.

## The rules

- **Cash first.** When two ideas look equal, the one closer to money wins.
- **Approval before it goes out.** Money, prices, contracts and hiring need a yes before anything is sent. Office messages start with «🧭 مكتب التشغيل».
- **Argue, then decide.** Every rule on these pages can be challenged. Thursday's review states the strongest case for and against, decides, and writes down why.

## When this happens

- **You need a decision today** → ask in the group: the choice in one line, plus what you recommend. Easy to undo? Decide and say so. Money, price, contract or people? It waits for a yes.
- **You are stuck** → say so in the group the same day, and what you need.
- **You disagree with a page or a rule** → say so in the group, in your own words. Thursday's review argues both sides and changes the page the same day, or tells you why not.
- **You missed the Sunday plan** → answer it as soon as you see it. Tuesday's check-in asks again.

## What you do

Answer the Sunday plan the same day. Stuck, or disagree? Say so in the group.

## References

Where this page's way of working comes from:

- Grove (1983), [*High Output Management*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/72467/high-output-management-by-andrew-s-grove/) — a leader's output is the team's output, and a fixed rhythm of short meetings keeps it moving. Our Sunday, Tuesday and Thursday.
- Bezos (2016), [2015 Letter to Shareholders](https://s2.q4cdn.com/299287126/files/doc_financials/annual/2015-Letter-to-Shareholders.PDF), Amazon — reversible decisions belong to small groups and should be fast; only the irreversible ones go up. Why the office decides only what is hard to undo.
- Doerr (2018), [*Measure What Matters*](https://www.whatmatters.com/the-book) — a few goals, each with a number, checked every week. The numbers the office tracks.
