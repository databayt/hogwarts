## The knowledge-piece formula

1. A **hook** that opens a knowledge gap.
2. The problem or the common belief.
3. What the evidence actually says.
4. What the evidence does not prove.
5. What it means for a school.
6. One practical step.
7. A natural **product bridge**, if there is one.
8. One **CTA**.

## The product bridge

A bridge doesn't mean stuffing the balqalam name at the end of every post.

| Idea                         | Honest bridge to the product                          |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Active recall                | Exam design and results follow-up                     |
| Absence                      | Attendance and follow-up with the family              |
| Feedback                     | Results and messages arriving at the right time       |
| Spaced learning              | Timetable, lessons and progress tracking              |
| Student privacy              | Roles, isolation, audit log and export                |
| Staff cognitive load         | Operations in one place instead of hopping between files |

If there is no honest bridge, the piece is published to build trust — **with no forced link**.

## Tone

- Clear, intelligent, direct Arabic. We don't write like a typical company post.
- A scene or a question comes before the term, and every term is explained when used.
- No hype: no "revolution", "changes everything", "first with no competitor".
- For Sudan, an educated Sudanese register is fine; regional material uses simple Modern Standard Arabic.

## Research & editorial policy

### Source order

1. The original research or official document.
2. Systematic reviews and independent bodies that grade evidence.
3. Universities and official education bodies.
4. Serious science journalism to explain the news — always traced back to the original.
5. Social media accounts are never a source for a claim.

### Evidence labels

| Label                 | Meaning                                                    |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strong                | Backed by several reviews or stable findings               |
| Promising             | Good results, but limited context or scale                 |
| Preliminary           | One study or a preprint — presented as a possibility, not a fact |
| Misleading / myth     | Not enough support, or it distorts the finding             |

### Guarding against neuroscience marketing

We never publish:

- "This method rewires the student's brain."
- Simplified dopamine claims.
- Learning styles as fixed biological facts.
- A brain image as evidence in itself.
- Correlation presented as causation.
- A finding in adults presented as settled fact for children.

### Keep the layers apart

Write each one explicitly — keeping them apart is part of the brand:

- **The study found:** what the source supports.
- **This may mean for a school:** our professional interpretation.
- **In balqalam:** what the product actually does.

### Product review before publishing

- [ ] The name is balqalam, not Hogwarts.
- [ ] The feature exists in the [MVP](/docs/mvp).
- [ ] The number is in the [Claim Registry](/docs/marketing/claims).
- [ ] The country fits the claim.
- [ ] No promise of offline, App Store or advanced analytics.
- [ ] No undocumented case-study result.
- [ ] The CTA can be acted on today.
