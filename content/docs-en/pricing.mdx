## Why it pays

One public price makes every quote fast and every deal comparable. A per-student price grows with the school: a small school pays little, and when a school grows, so do we. The price is set against the full cost of the company, not only the servers: a price that covers hosting alone never pays the people who build it.

## The price

| Students | A year | First payment (50%) | Then 4 payments (12.5% each) |
| -------- | ------ | ------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 50       | $320   | $160                | $40                          |
| 100      | $640   | $320                | $80                          |
| 250      | $1,600 | $800                | $200                         |
| 500      | $3,200 | $1,600              | $400                         |

- No free plan, no bands, no setup fee.
- Over 1,000 students: the same rate, with its own contract.

No discounts protects the price for everyone. A discount given once becomes the price the next school asks for. If we ever run an offer, it is written as a share off the full price for a set time — "50% off the first year" — so the school always sees the real price. Sales and marketing propose offers to fit the market, and the CEO office approves them.

## Break-even, in students

Each paying student brings $0.53 a month ($6.40 ÷ 12). **Students needed = monthly cost ÷ $0.53.**

| What is covered                         | Per month    | Paying students            |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------ | -------------------------- |
| The platform                            | about $70    | about 130 (2–3 schools Al-Ibda's size) |
| The platform and Claude                 | about $170   | about 320                  |
| The full cost, with the team paid       | about $2,700 | about 5,000                |

The last row is the real target. It uses the team estimate of $2,500 a month from our pricing discussion on 4 October. Our first school, Al-Ibda, pays about $25.60 a month.

## How a school pays

- The free month ends, then the school pays 50%.
- Then 12.5% at months 3, 6, 9 and 12.
- Each payment counts the students on that day.
- In Sudan the price is in US dollars, paid in Sudanese pounds at the bank rate on the payment day, by Bankak.

## How to quote

1. Ask how many students the school has.
2. Multiply by $6.40, or use the calculator at balqalam.com/pricing.
3. Send the total and the payment plan in one message.

## When this happens

- **"It's too expensive"** → ask what they spend today, in money and in staff hours, on fees, attendance and the timetable. Then offer the free month to judge it. Never cut the price on the call.
- **"Can you give us a discount?"** → "Our price is the same for every school. The first month is free, so you can judge it first."
- **The school has more than 1,000 students** → the same rate, its own contract. Send the deal to the CEO office.
- **A school outside Sudan asks** → quote 24 SAR per student a year, and send the deal to the CEO office for how it pays.

## References

Where this page's way of working comes from:

- Nagle & Müller (2017), [*The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing*, 6th ed.](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315185309) — cost sets the floor, value sets the price, and discounts won by bargaining teach every buyer to bargain. Our full-cost floor and the no-discount rule.
- Ramanujam & Tacke (2016), [*Monetizing Innovation*](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Monetizing+Innovation-p-9781119240860) — design the price early, and charge by a unit that grows with the value the customer gets. Per student.
- Graham (2015), ["Default Alive or Default Dead?"](http://paulgraham.com/aord.html) — a price is only right if it gets the company to profit before the money runs out. Why break-even is counted in students.
