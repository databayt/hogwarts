Three shapes of proof are in play, and they are not interchangeable:

- **Install base** — schools, students, countries. The default. Requires scale we do not have.
- **Outcome** — Arbor alone quantifies the benefit ("117+ minutes saved per staff member weekly", "74% better working life"). Far more persuasive to an overloaded administrator than a headcount, and available to anyone willing to measure.
- **Operational** — EduX's "48-hour activation", Skyward's "6-week implementation". Cheap to state, instantly checkable, and the only category a new entrant can win outright.

## CTA gating

The single most exploitable gap in the category. Sorted by how much friction stands between a curious school and the product.

| Door                   | Vendors                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sales-gated only       | PowerSchool, FACTS, Infinite Campus, Veracross, Classter, Arbor, iSAMS, Tes Engage, Bromcom, ParentPay Group, Classera, Skolera, Alef, Fedena |
| Video before contact   | Skyward ("Watch the story")                                                                                                                   |
| Self-serve trial       | EduX (30-day), Edusys ("Sign up for free"), openSIS ("Start Free Trial", alongside a demo request)                                            |
| Download and self-host | Gibbon, RosarioSIS, Frappe Education                                                                                                          |

**14 of 20 have no self-serve path at all.** A school cannot see the product without booking a call and sitting through a pipeline. The three commercial vendors offering a trial are EduX, Edusys and openSIS — and EduX is the one positioned closest to us.

A permanently free tier, with no trial clock and no card, is stronger than every door in this table. It is a structural advantage over the whole category and belongs in the hero rather than three scrolls down.

## Pricing transparency

Nobody in the category puts a price on the landing page.

| Vendor        | What is published                                                                           |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Edusys        | A range, buried in a FAQ: "1 to 5 AED per student/month or annually 4,000 AED/Year or more" |
| openSIS       | Pricing model named but not priced — "staff-based pricing"                                  |
| Fedena        | A "Pricing & Plans" link in the hero, price behind it                                       |
| Everyone else | Nothing. Quote on request.                                                                  |

Publishing a real per-student number is therefore a category-level differentiator on its own, before anyone compares the number itself.

<Callout
  type="warning"
  title="Internal — the number is not automatically the win"
>
  Edusys anchors Gulf buyers at **1–5 AED per student per month**. Pro at $1.50
  is ≈5.5 AED — at or just above the top of that band. Lead with the free tier
  and with what the price includes (every module, no per-module upsell), not
  with "we're cheaper", which is not true against a Gulf ERP reseller. Canonical
  tiers: [Business model](/docs/business-model).
</Callout>

## Section order

The category has converged on one skeleton. Counts are out of the 20 pages captured.

| #   | Section                   | Pages using it | Note                                                                      |
| --- | ------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Hero — headline + CTA     | 20             | Universal                                                                 |
| 2   | Proof band                | 18             | Scale numbers, customer logos, or both                                    |
| 3   | Who it's for              | 14             | By school type, or by role (leader / teacher / parent / student)          |
| 4   | "Everything in one place" | 13             | The anti-add-on pitch; Bromcom, Skolera and Classter each build a section |
| 5   | Module grid               | 17             | SIS, finance, HR, transport, library, comms                               |
| 6   | Testimonials              | 15             | Usually a carousel                                                        |
| 7   | Named case studies        | 11             | Best ones carry a school name and a student count                         |
| 8   | Content marketing         | 9              | Blog, podcast, annual report, events                                      |
| 9   | Awards / partners         | 8              | Classter and Alef both lead with awards                                   |
| 10  | Closing CTA               | 20             | Almost always "book a demo"                                               |

Steps 1, 5 and 10 are effectively mandatory. Steps 8 and 9 are what large vendors add once they have run out of product claims.

## Trust devices

How the category answers "can we rely on you?" without being asked. Each of these maps to a fear catalogued in the [FAQ](/docs/competitors/faq).

- **Named case studies with a student count.** "British International School of Jeddah, 1,300+ students" does more work than any feature list. Eleven vendors run these; they are the highest-value asset we currently lack.
- **Longevity claims.** FACTS "40+ years", Bromcom "40+ years", ParentPay "40+ years", Skyward "since 1980", openSIS "18+ years". This is a direct answer to _will you still exist in five years_ — the question we are least able to answer with age, and must answer with architecture instead.
- **Security and compliance documents.** Bromcom links an Information Security Policy Statement and a Cloud Self Certification straight from the footer. Post-2025 this moved from procurement paperwork to landing-page furniture.
- **Awards.** Alef replaces its hero with a TIME ranking; Classter runs a full awards section and a "98% user satisfaction" badge.
- **Total-cost calculators.** Bromcom publishes a TCO calculator — pre-empting the hidden-cost objection by inviting the arithmetic.

## Regional and language patterns

- **EduX names its geography in the headline** ("built for MENA, Gulf & Africa") and lists nine countries in the subheadline, then adds ministry-compliant reporting, local payment gateways and a native parent app. That is our positioning, written by someone else, on the page, first.
- **Bilingual is a stated feature, not an implied one.** EduX puts "AR / EN" in the proof band. Fedena claims 20+ languages, Gibbon 26+. None of them treat Arabic as the default rendering direction — which is a real difference, but it is a _product_ difference we currently fail to claim on the page.
- **Ministry compliance is a headline-level concern in MENA and invisible in the UK/US sets.** Edusys and EduX both surface it early; no global incumbent mentions a ministry anywhere.

## Selling time

Four of the twenty sell time rather than features, and it is the most persuasive copy in the set:

- Arbor — "Choose a better way to work" · 117+ minutes saved per staff member weekly
- Skyward — "Less time on tasks. More time for students."
- ParentPay Group — "Creating more time for learning"
- Veracross — "School data chaos, solved."

Our own marketing already believes this: `marketing.missionCards` in `src/components/internationalization/en.json` reads "A restoration of time, elimination of repetitive… Schools lose 40 hours monthly to paperwork; we give 80% of that back." That claim is stronger than anything in the table above — and it still sits in section 3, below the fold. The hero itself stopped being abstract on 2026-08-30, when "Automate the boring, elevate the wonder." gave way to "The complete platform for schools and teaching." / `المنظومة الشاملة لإدارة المدارس والعملية التعليمية`.

## What to steal

- **The proof band.** Universal, and ours is written already. Make it honest and checkable — modules shipped, languages, payment rails, hours to a live subdomain — rather than an inflated install base we would have to defend.
- **The outcome number over the headcount.** We cannot win 60M students. We can win "117 minutes" — Arbor's own framing, aimed at the same overloaded administrator, and measurable from our own usage data.
- **The named case study.** The single highest-leverage asset we do not have. Worth asking a pilot school for consent explicitly.
- **The operational proof.** "Subdomain in 24 hours" is EduX's "48-hour activation" halved, and it is true today.
- **Geography in the headline.** EduX proved it works and got there first on the page, even though we get there first in the product.

## What to avoid

- **"Book a demo" as the only door.** Fourteen vendors made this mistake and it is why a self-serve free tier cuts through.
- **The superlative arms race.** "World's most comprehensive", "world's premium", "the ultimate in school efficiency", "the best". The category is saturated with unfalsifiable claims — which is exactly why one concrete, checkable claim stands out.
- **Awards as a substitute for a hero.** Alef replaced its headline with a TIME ranking. Nobody evaluating a school platform came to read a press release.
- **Numbers we cannot defend.** "700+ School automated" is currently in the dictionary and unverified. Publishing an install-base number we would lose an argument over costs more than publishing none.
