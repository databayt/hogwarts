## Why it pays

The CRM is how we know how much money is coming, and when. Every Trial deal carries its amount and its next date, so Thursday's review can add up the next payments. A deal that lives only in your WhatsApp is invisible to everyone but you.

## Where

- **hogwarts.databayt.org** — sign in with your name@databayt.org.
- It holds about 3,900 schools we collected. Most are untouched leads; only real conversations become deals.
- If it does not open, tell Abdout.

## The stages we use

| Stage     | Means                              |
| --------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Contacted | We spoke with the school           |
| Demo      | They saw Hogwarts working          |
| Trial     | Their free month is running        |
| Paid      | Their first payment arrived        |
| Lost      | They said no — write why           |

When a school reaches Trial, open a deal with its amount and its next date.

The stages match the six steps of [Sales](/docs/sales), so counting deals at each stage shows where schools stop.

## Three rules

1. Log every real conversation the same day.
2. Every deal has a next step and a date.
3. The person who talks to the school owns the deal.

## When this happens

- **You met a school in person or by phone** → log it the same day as Contacted: what they said, and the next date.
- **A deal has no next step for seven days** → it is stalling. Call, or move it to Lost with the reason. Ali flags these every Thursday.
- **The school says "call us next month"** → set the next date for that day. The CRM reminds you, not your memory.
- **You hand a school to someone else** → change the owner, and add one line of context.

## What you do

- **Ali** cleans the CRM every Thursday, before the review.
- Everyone: no deal lives only in WhatsApp.

## References

Where this page's way of working comes from:

- Rackham (1988), [*SPIN Selling*](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/product/spin-selling-rackham/M9780070511132.html) — a call succeeds only when the buyer agrees to a next step (an "advance"), not when they say "sounds good". A deal moves stage only on an advance.
- Ross & Tyler (2011), [*Predictable Revenue*](https://www.predictablerevenue.com/book) — a written pipeline with clear stages and one owner per deal makes revenue predictable.
