**Every sale has six steps. Write each step in the CRM.**

## Why it pays

Sales is the only page that brings money in directly. Each school pays $6.40 a year for each student, so a 300-student school is about $1,900 a year. Speed matters as much as the pitch: a school that hears back within the hour is far likelier to talk than one that hears back tomorrow.

## The six steps

1. **Find** — a school owner or principal. A school group with branches is best.
2. **Talk** — ask the five questions below.
3. **Demo** — open balqalam.com on their phone and press "Try the platform".
4. **Free month** — we set up their school with them.
5. **Contract** — one page, signed by Abdout.
6. **Paid** — the first payment arrives.

Each step is a stage in the [CRM](/docs/crm), so anyone can see where every school stands.

## Five questions

1. How many students, and how many branches?
2. What do you use today?
3. What takes most of your time: fees, attendance, the timetable, parents?
4. Who decides, and when?
5. Can you start this month?

They ask what the school does today, not what it might buy. Past behaviour is honest; compliments are not.

## The demo, in five minutes

Start with their answer to question 3, then show the school's own day in this order: attendance → fees and invoices → the timetable → messages to parents → live classes. End with one question: "Shall we set up your school this week?"

## Two WhatsApp messages

- **First:** "Hello, I'm [name] from Balqalam — one system for the whole school: admissions, timetable, attendance, live classes and fees, in Arabic. May I send you a link to try it? The first month is free."
- **Three days later:** "Hello [name], did you try the link? I can set up your school this week."

Send the Arabic version to Sudanese schools.

## Groups first

One contract with a school group brings many schools. Ask every school: do you have branches?

## When this happens

- **"We use Excel" or "we use paper"** → "Keep it for the month. Try us beside it; the first month is free."
- **"It's too expensive"** → the answer is on [Pricing](/docs/pricing): the free month first, then the per-student price. Never a discount.
- **"Our internet is weak"** → run the demo on their own phone and their own network. Never say it works offline.
- **"Is our data safe?"** → "Your data is yours. You can export it any time, and we keep it private. It is in the contract."
- **"We already have a system"** → ask what it does not do (question 3), and show only that.
- **The school goes quiet** → send the second message after three days, then one last message a week later. Still nothing? Mark it Lost with the reason, and try again next term.
- **The school says yes** → that day, move it to Trial in the CRM and tell tech to start the week-one setup on [Support desk](/docs/support-desk).

## Argue with this page

- **The case against:** a cold WhatsApp message to an owner can feel like spam. An introduction from someone they trust may convert far better.
- **What would change it:** fewer than 2 demos from 20 first messages. Then the first step becomes introductions, through partners and school-owner communities.

## References

- Fitzpatrick (2013), [*The Mom Test*](https://www.momtestbook.com/) — ask about what people did, not what they think they will do; compliments are not data.
- Rackham (1988), [*SPIN Selling*](https://www.routledge.com/SPINr-Selling/Rackham/p/book/9781138465954) — from a study of more than 35,000 sales calls: in large sales, the winners ask about the problem and its consequences more than they pitch.
- Oldroyd, McElheran & Elkington (2011), ["The Short Life of Online Sales Leads"](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads), Harvard Business Review — firms that replied within an hour were nearly 7× likelier to qualify the lead.
- Cialdini (1984), [*Influence*](https://www.influenceatwork.com/) — when unsure, people do what similar others did. One school's real story sells to the next school.
