One self-contained block at `src/components/school-dashboard/live/`, mirrored 1:1 to the
`/live` route. It runs three meeting back-ends behind a single UI: the **external
pasted-link** provider is live everywhere; the **LiveKit SFU** (in-app rooms + recording) is fully
coded and gated only on four `LIVEKIT_*` env vars — see RUNBOOK.md for the LiveKit Cloud quick path; recording is a separate gate and no longer blocks a room; the **native Meet / Zoom / Teams** adapters create
real meetings via each vendor's API but ship dark until OAuth credentials land.

Three things are worth reading twice:

- **Going online never closes the building.** Online is something you _add_ to a
  class, not something that replaces it. A school can be open and online on the
  same day.
- **Nobody schedules classes one at a time.** The school says how it teaches;
  the timetable it already keeps becomes the live classes, every morning, on
  its own.
- **Attendance and recordings look after themselves.** Nobody ticks a register,
  and nobody uploads a video.

## Usage guide

### If you run the school

1. **Say how the school teaches.** _School → Configuration → Live classes_ (the
   same panel is the **Settings** tab inside Live classes). Pick **In person**,
   **Online**, or **Hybrid**. In person means no live rooms at all, whatever any
   section says — everything below only matters for the other two.
2. **Choose the meeting back-end.** **In-app room** keeps the class inside the
   app — recording, attendance, whiteboard, the lot. **External link** uses
   Google Meet / Zoom / Teams URLs and works with no infrastructure. You can
   pick the in-app room before it is provisioned; classes quietly run on
   external links until it lands, then switch over on their own.
3. **If you are on external links, give classes somewhere to go.** Set a
   **standing meeting link** as the floor — every class without a link of its
   own lands there — and add a link per class over time. **Meeting-link
   coverage** on the same page tells you how far you have got.
4. **Decide what a class is allowed to be.** Recording on or off, how long
   recordings are kept, the notice students see when a class is recorded, and
   whether recordings are published to the lesson automatically. Then: may
   guardians watch, do students enter muted, and which room tools are on —
   chat, raising hands, polls, the whiteboard, letting students share a screen.
5. **Set the timing.** How many minutes before a class the reminder goes out,
   and — if you want attendance marked automatically — when a student counts as
   late, as present, and as having left early.
6. **Tune the exceptions.** In hybrid, **by grade** and **by section** override
   the school default in both directions. "School default" means inherit.
7. **When something closes the school**, use **Go online temporarily** with a
   start date and, if you know it, an end date. Today's classes are created
   while you are still on the page. Clearing the start date ends it — there is
   no separate cancel button to forget.

### If you teach

1. **You do not create your classes.** They appear for every slot on your
   timetable, each morning.
2. **Start from where you already are** — the Join button on your timetable's
   Today card, or the Live classes dashboard. Starting the class is what opens
   the room for everyone else.
3. **The class opens on its own card** — the subject's artwork, the grade, who
   is teaching, when it runs, and one **Join** button. Nothing is started and
   nobody is called into the room until you press it, so you can open a class
   to look at it without starting it. Over the artwork sit **Back**, **ADD** —
   which saves the class to your own calendar — and **share**, which hands the
   class's link to whoever you pick.
4. **The page carries on under the card.** The card stops short of the bottom
   of the screen on purpose — four fifths of it, on a phone as on a laptop.
   Below it are four rows: the rest of the section's day, each class opening in
   its room when it is running and offering its recording when there is one;
   the exams, assignments and links attached to this class; the subject's own
   self-study lessons; and the teacher with the students the class is taught
   to.
5. **In the room** you have your microphone and camera, screen share, a
   whiteboard, any PDF attached to the lesson as slides with the page synced to
   everyone, polls, a questions list you can mark answered, the raised-hands
   queue, and the ability to remove someone.
6. **You can change one class** without changing the school: the class form has
   its own **Students enter** setting — school default, muted, or mic and
   camera on.
7. **End the class** when you are done. Attendance is written from the time each
   student spent in the room, across drop-outs and reconnects. If the class was
   recorded, the session page shows the recording being prepared, then ready.

### If you are a student

1. **Join appears where your day already is** — the Today rows of your
   timetable, the Current/Next card, the Live classes page, and the reminder
   notification. It shows up when the class is running, or about to.
2. **You enter muted with your camera off** unless the teacher opened the class
   differently. Turn them on when you are asked to.
3. **Raise your hand, ask a question, answer a poll, or use the chat** —
   whichever of those your school has switched on.
4. **A weak connection is handled for you.** The picture steps down before it
   breaks, and finally to audio with the slides. If you drop out, you are
   offered _Rejoin_ rather than being thrown out.
5. **If you missed it and the class was recorded**, it shows up as the lesson's
   video. It plays in the app; it cannot be downloaded, and it carries a faint
   watermark with your name.

### If you are a parent

Open the **Parent portal → your child → Timetable**. Today's classes carry the
same Join, and you enter as an observer — you can watch and listen, but not
speak, use the camera, or ask questions. Your school can switch this off
entirely.

## Data flow

```
landing (/live)          landing/content.tsx (server) → hero banner · live strip · catch-up shelf · recordings pair · readiness · role guide
   └─ View sessions
index (/live/dashboard)  content.tsx (server) → table.tsx (client) → form.tsx (modal create)
   └─ row Join / detail
detail (/live/[id])      detail.tsx → Join (live/scheduled) | Recordings (ended)
   └─ Join
room  (/live/[id]/room)  joinLiveClass → eligibility → 5-min JWT → room.tsx (LiveKit prebuilt)
                                              ↑ refreshLiveClassToken every ~60s re-runs eligibility
recordings (/[id]/recordings)  recordings.tsx → recording-player.tsx (signed S3 URL, 4h; re-mints on expiry via onError)
```

- **Session state machine** (`actions/sessions.ts`): `scheduled → live → ended`, plus `cancelled`
  and `failed`. `startLiveClass` provisions the SFU room (checks `conferenceMaxConcurrent`);
  `endLiveClass` is idempotent. The **webhook is the authoritative writer** for `live`
  (`room_started`) and `ended` (`room_finished`).
- **Eligibility** is resolved lazily per join (`actions/tokens.ts`): TEACHER→HOST (own session) /
  CO_HOST, STUDENT→PARTICIPANT (section match), GUARDIAN→OBSERVER (child in section), ADMIN→CO_HOST,
  DEVELOPER→HOST. Kicked users (`status="removed"`) are blocked at the next token refresh.
- **Recordings** (`pending → processing → ready → expired`): LiveKit Egress → S3 `me-central-1`;
  per-school retention enforced by the daily cron.
- **Notifications** (`actions/notifications.ts`): all 5 `live_class_*` events resolve their audience
  (teacher + section roster + guardians) and dispatch through the shared hub
  (`dispatchNotificationsToAudience`), so they reach the **in-app bell and email**, honour per-user
  channel preferences, and carry an absolute action URL. Both the in-app room path (`sessions.ts`) and
  the external pasted-link path (`list-actions.ts` create / reschedule / cancel) fan out.
- **Attendance** (`actions/attendance-sync.ts`, opt-in via `School.conferenceAttendanceSync`): when a
  session ends (webhook `room_finished` or the stale-session cron), `syncConferenceAttendance` marks
  each section student PRESENT/LATE from their in-room presence and ABSENT for roster non-joiners,
  writing `Attendance` rows with method `VIRTUAL`. Requires a `sectionId` + `timetableId`; **LiveKit
  only** — external links carry no presence data. Idempotent (section unique key, revive-on-update).

`(app)` is a route **group**: it adds no URL segment, only the shared heading and tab strip
(Sessions · Schedule · Settings · Network test, narrowed by role). `/live` and
`/live/[id]` sit outside it — the landing page owns its own hero, and a session detail is a
leaf reached from a row rather than a tab.

The room route lives under a separate **`(live-room)`** group with a bare full-viewport layout (no
sidebar / dashboard chrome) so the SFU stream owns the screen; it is `force-dynamic` for OOM-safe
builds. API (external / scheduler-invoked / polled → stay routes, not server actions): `api/webhooks/livekit`
(HMAC), `api/live/token` (the in-room ~4-min token refresh — a GET route because a server-action
poll would ship a full RSC re-render per response), `api/cron/live-class-reminders` (every 15 min,
5–20-min idempotent window), `api/cron/end-stale-live-classes` (every 30 min — closes sessions stuck
`live` past their end + runs the attendance sync), `api/cron/expire-live-recordings` (daily).

### Access control — private vs school-wide

`visibility` decides who may see and join a session, on top of the absolute tenant boundary:

- **`section`** (default) — the session's section roster joins as PARTICIPANT, their guardians as
  OBSERVER; a session with no section is host-only (a private meeting).
- **`school`** — every member of the school: students PARTICIPANT, guardians OBSERVER,
  staff/accountant PARTICIPANT. For assemblies, PD webinars, parent town halls. Scheduling one
  notifies the whole school through the notification hub.

There is deliberately **no cross-school or anonymous tier** — eligibility, list scoping, detail
reads, and recordings access all enforce the same rule (`resolveParticipantRole`,
`canAccessSession`, `buildLiveClassWhere`). Meeting and reference URLs are scheme-locked to
http(s) — `javascript:`/`data:` URIs are rejected at validation.

### References — lesson, quiz, assignment, links

One `catalogLessonId` FK links a session to a catalog lesson; the session page then surfaces the
lesson's **videos, attachments, materials, and practice-question count** automatically.
Quizzes/exams (`SchoolExam` — a quiz is `examType: QUIZ`, not a separate model), assignments, and
ad-hoc links attach as `ConferenceResource` rows — tenant-verified before write, replaced as a set
on update. The wizard's References step loads its pickers per chosen subject
(`getLiveClassReferenceOptions`, staff-gated).

### The create wizard

The dashboard form is a 5-step compact wizard (`form.tsx` + `form-steps.tsx`) on the house
stepped-modal pattern — **Basics → Schedule → Meeting → References → Access**, a handful of fields
per step.

Basics opens with the **Class** picker: the school's real timetable slots for the active term
("Mathematics · Grade 1-A · Sunday 08:00"). This is what makes a session the _online delivery of a
physical class_ rather than a standalone meeting — picking a slot fills in the teacher, subject,
section, the period's time window and the next date that weekday falls on, and stores the
`timetableId`. That anchor is load-bearing: it is what lets the session **mark attendance** from who
actually joins (the sync needs a section roster _and_ a period) and what makes the class show up as
a live/scheduled dot on the weekly timetable grid. The server re-derives teacher/subject/section
from the slot row, so the schedule — not the form — is the source of truth. Leave the picker on
_"Not tied to a class"_ for assemblies, parent town halls and one-off tutorials. The anchor cannot
be changed later (re-anchoring would re-key attendance that has already been written).

The subject and lesson pickers follow the **catalog, scoped to the section's grade**: subjects are
the ones this school adopted for that grade (shown under the school's own name for them), and
catalog lessons come from that grade's chapters. Every session is created `scheduled`; going live
and ending are separate, guarded steps.

The Meeting step picks the back-end: **In-app room** (LiveKit — default whenever
`isLiveKitConfigured()`, with recording + max-participants knobs) or **External link** (pasted
Meet/Zoom/Teams URL + optional weekly-recurring default). Provider is immutable after creation;
Join is provider-aware everywhere (table menu, detail page, and the room route hands external
sessions to their vendor URL).

## Teaching online

The first setting is how the school teaches — **in person**, **online**, or
**hybrid** — under _School → Configuration → Live classes_ (the same panel is
the Settings tab of Live classes). In person means no live rooms and no join
buttons, whatever any section says. Online means every timetable slot is a
live class. Hybrid is where the choices below live: a default for sections,
per-section overrides, and a temporary go-online window. Changing to in person
applies to new sessions; the day's already-scheduled classes stay and close
on their own.

Beyond the mode, the same panel holds: the meeting back-end and standing link;
how online classes run (timetable, open room, both); recording on/off,
retention, the notice students see on joining a recorded class, and whether
recordings are published to the lesson automatically; whether guardians may
watch; whether students enter muted (a class can override it); which room
tools are on — chat, raising hands, polls, the whiteboard, student screen
share; how many minutes before a class the reminder goes out; attendance
thresholds; per-grade and per-section online overrides; and a read-only
statement of the content-protection policy with recording storage used.

A school that delivers its classes online does not schedule them one at a time.
It declares the intent once, and every timetable slot becomes a live class.

Going online never closes the building. Online delivery is **additive** — a
live channel opened alongside the physical class — so a school can be open and
online on the same day, online on Sunday and in person on Monday, or online for
one section and not another. Nothing here cancels a lesson.

Three independent switches can put a class online, and they combine rather than
compete:

| Switch                         | Where                   | Means                                                                       |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Teach online**               | `/live/settings`        | The standing "we are an online school" setting.                             |
| **Online teaching by section** | same page, per section  | Overrides the school default in both directions. "School default" inherits. |
| **Go online temporarily**      | same page, a date range | The emergency switch — see below. Lifts the school default for those days.  |

The per-section decision always wins over the two school-wide ones, including
during an emergency: the window raises the school's _default_, it does not
overrule a choice someone made about a section. A section held back that you
now want online is one click — set it back to "School default".

### Going online at short notice

Weather, a closed road, an outbreak, a war. `/live/settings` →
**Go online temporarily**:

- **From** — the first day. Required.
- **Until** — the last day, **inclusive**. Leave it empty to stay online _until
  further notice_, which is usually what you know on day one.
- **Reason** — free text, shown to staff on this page.

Both dates are read in the school's own timezone and cover whole school days.
Clearing **From** ends the period — there is no separate "cancel" button to
forget. When the end date passes, the school simply goes back to whatever it
was doing, with no manual step.

Saving takes effect immediately: today's classes are created while you are
still on the page, rather than at the next quarter-hour.

### Timetable-bound or free timing

**How online classes run** is a separate choice from whether they run:

- **Follow the timetable** — one live class per timetable slot, starting and
  ending with the period. The strict version, and the one that feeds period
  attendance.
- **Open room, any time** — one standing room per section for the whole
  teaching day, with no period boundaries. A school that went online because of
  a storm rarely reproduces its bell schedule on day one, and a school that is
  online by design may not want one at all. Hosted by the section's homeroom
  teacher. Because it is not tied to a period, it does not write period
  attendance, and it does not appear on the timetable cards either — open rooms
  are reached from **Conference**.
- **Timetable + open room** — both, so a section can follow its schedule and
  still have somewhere to meet in the gaps.

### Set it up

1. **Choose the back-end.** `/live/settings` (ADMIN / DEVELOPER) →
   **Teach online**, then **Meeting back-end**:
   - **External link** — Google Meet / Zoom / Teams URLs. Works today, no infra.
   - **In-app room** — LiveKit. Selectable immediately, but it only takes
     effect once the SFU is provisioned (the six gates in
     `conference/RUNBOOK.md`). Until then classes quietly run on external
     links and switch over on their own the day the infra lands — no
     migration, no re-setup.
2. **For external links, set one link per class.** Create a session from the
   wizard with **Save as default**, which stores a recurring `ConferenceLink`
   for that (subject, section, term). This is where a materialized class gets
   its meeting URL — a slot with no link is skipped rather than published with
   a dead Join button. At term rollover, **Carry forward recurring links** on
   the same settings page clones them into the next term.

   **Meeting-link coverage** on the same page tells you how far you have got:
   how many (section, subject) pairs in the active term have their own link,
   and which do not. Check it before you need it.

3. **Set a standing meeting link as the floor.** A school going online at short
   notice will not have a link per class ready. **Standing meeting link** is
   used for any class that has none of its own, so the flip produces joinable
   classes instead of nothing at all. It is **one shared room** — every class
   falling back to it lands in the same meeting — so treat it as the emergency
   floor and add per-class links as you go. An open room has no other source of
   a URL, since it belongs to no subject.
4. **Tune per section if the school is hybrid.** The **Online teaching by
   section** list overrides the school-wide switch in both directions: hold one
   section back from a school that went online, or put one section online in a
   school that has not. "School default" means inherit.

### What then happens

Every 15 minutes the reminders cron materializes the current school day's
online slots — one `Conference` row per slot, per day, in the school's own
timezone. It mirrors the timetable's own filter (active term, that weekday,
`weekOffset: 0`, no break periods, slots that have both a section and a
teacher), so it can never invent a class the schedule doesn't show. Re-runs are
no-ops, and a class an admin cancelled is **not** resurrected.

Nothing is created on a day the school has declared closed — a HOLIDAY or
CANCELLED entry in the schedule exceptions stops the sweep for that day, so
nobody is reminded about a class on Eid. The timetable itself still shows the
day, with a "school is closed today" notice over it: holiday entries are typed
by hand, and a wrong one should not make a whole timetable disappear with no
explanation. Periods that have already finished are skipped too, so flipping
the switch at lunchtime does not publish Join buttons for the morning.

Because a real row exists before anyone clicks, the rest of the product wakes
up: students get the start-reminder notification, the weekly grid shows the
scheduled/live dot on the slot, every timetable card marks the class
**Online** — beside the room, not instead of it, because the room is still
where the class meets for anyone who can get there — the teacher's Current card
offers **Start**,
and — on the LiveKit path with attendance sync enabled — presence is written
back as `VIRTUAL` attendance when the class ends. Sessions nobody ever starts
are closed as `cancelled` by the end-stale cron rather than lingering.

Each session's own page says how attendance is being handled for it — marked
automatically when it ends, or to be marked as usual, with a link to the
register. External meetings carry no attendance data at all, which is the
common case for a school that has just gone online, so it is stated rather than
left to be discovered from an empty register.

| Attendance from presence    | ⛔ no presence data | ✅ opt-in                            |
| In-room moderation (kick)   | ⛔                  | ✅                                   |

## How a student joins

Five surfaces reach a live class, and they differ in how tightly they gate on time:

| Surface                                                 | Gate                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Timetable **Today** rows (student · teacher · guardian) | in progress, or starting within 10 min (`isRowLiveJoinable`)                                |
| Timetable **Current/Next** card                         | the current class, or the next within 10 min (`isLiveJoinable`)                             |
| `/live` landing "now" strip                             | live now + upcoming, section-scoped                                                         |
| `/live/dashboard` table                                 | any `live` or `scheduled` row the viewer can see                                            |
| **Parent portal** → child → Timetable                   | a "Today" strip above the weekly table                                                      |
| Notification                                            | `started` links straight to the room; the rest to the session detail                        |
| **Mobile**                                              | `live_class` on `/api/mobile/timetable/:userId`, joined via `GET /api/mobile/live/:id/join` |

The timetable weekly grid deliberately shows only a live dot — awareness, not a Join affordance.

A LiveKit session opens the in-app room at `/live/[id]/room`; an external session opens its
vendor URL directly. The room route redirects external sessions after the enrollment-gated read, so
either provider works from every surface above.

**Only the `started` notification deep-links to the room.** `startingSoon` fires 5–20 minutes before
the class, and a non-HOST joining a still-`scheduled` session is refused — an early room link would
land a student on an error rather than a waiting room.

## In the room

The room is the app's own — every control is labelled from the dictionary and
the layout follows the page direction. Its chrome is the lesson player's phone
layout, the way Apple's video player lays it out, with the class's own
controls in the player's slots:

- **Top start:** leave (✕), the people list (teacher only) and fit / full
  screen, in one glass pill. **Top end:** the connection, tinted by its
  quality; tap it for the delivery tiers.
- **The bottom card:** the class's clock — a read-only progress bar with the
  time elapsed and the time left — and one row of five: the discussion panel
  (chat, questions, poll, hands, with a count), camera, the **microphone** in
  the centre, raise hand (teacher: share screen), and `⋯` for the rest —
  whiteboard, slides, devices.
- The chrome fades three seconds after the last touch and comes back on a tap
  of the picture; an open menu or panel keeps it up.

What a class can do in it:

- **Teacher:** microphone, camera, screen share, whiteboard, slides (any PDF
  attached to the lesson, page-synced to everyone), polls, a questions list
  with "mark answered", the raised-hands queue, and moderation (remove).
- **Student:** microphone and camera (both off on entry), raise hand, ask a
  question, vote, chat. Guardians observe only.
- **Delivery quality** adapts to the student's connection: a sustained poor
  patch steps the teacher's video down (720 → 360 → 180) and finally into
  **audio + slides** — the banner says so — and a sustained good stretch
  steps it back up. A manual choice in the Quality menu overrides the ladder.
- **Reconnecting** shows an overlay; a lost connection offers _Rejoin_ instead
  of ejecting. Being removed by the teacher, the class ending, or the room
  being opened on another device each say exactly that.
- **Attendance** is recorded from the time spent in the room, across
  reconnects — nothing to tap.
- Closed polls and every question are kept on the session as events.

### After the class

When recording is on, the finished recording is published as the lesson's
video in Lumos (visible to the school, downloadable), and the session page
moves through _recording is being prepared_ → _recording available_ — or
says plainly that the recording failed.

### Protecting videos and materials

Recordings, lesson videos and materials are watched inside the app and never
downloaded: there is no download control, no picture-in-picture or casting,
and materials open in an in-app viewer rather than as files. Every one of
those surfaces carries a faint, moving watermark with the viewer's identity,
so a screenshot or a phone recording can be traced. A student's own work —
playback position, quiz answers, an assignment hand-in — still queues offline
and syncs when the connection returns; `/offline` shows what is waiting.

## Operating it

- **The SFU**: four env vars (`LIVEKIT_HOST`, `LIVEKIT_WS_URL`, `LIVEKIT_API_KEY`,
  `LIVEKIT_API_SECRET`). Recording is a separate gate (`LIVEKIT_RECORDING_BUCKET` plus S3
  credentials) and no longer blocks holding a call. `/live/network-test` reports both.
- **A configured SFU does nothing on its own** — `School.conferenceProviderDefault` is `external`
  until a school opts in on `/live/settings`.
- **The materialization sweep runs on GitHub Actions**, not Vercel Cron, while the project is on the
  free plan (`.github/workflows/conference-crons.yml`). Without it an online school materializes no
  sessions after the day it saved its settings — nothing to join, and no reminders.

## Demo and the hybrid school

`pnpm db:seed:single conference` turns the demo into a working hybrid school: online by default
over LiveKit, two sections in person, five days of history with real presence and VIRTUAL
attendance, next-day sessions the cron will find as `exists`, a substitute hosting one of them, a
lesson session carrying an exam, an assignment and a link, three recurring external links, and a
declared holiday. It repairs first — break periods, teacher expertise, and a teacher on every
teacherless slot — because a slot with no teacher has no host and never goes online.

What "hybrid" means in the product: online is **additive**. The session detail shows the physical
room the class still meets in; the timetable card shows the room with an Online marker beside it;
a confirmed substitute hosts the online arm of the class they cover; a lumos lesson shows its live
session; and the role home pages carry Join. Recording stays honest — every switch says so when
there is no bucket.

## Decisions

- **One block, one provider abstraction.** External is the floor every school gets; LiveKit is the
  opt-in premium ceiling, gated by `isLiveKitConfigured()`. LiveKit's SFU lifecycle stays out of the
  `providers/` adapter layer (it's room-based, not link-based).
- **The now strip is one lead card and two brief ones.** The first card is whatever is running,
  or the next class to start, and it carries everything: subject, chapter, lesson, the teacher with
  their portrait, and where the class is in its own clock. The two under it are two text rows —
  the subject with its grade badge, then the teacher and the time on one line. The hierarchy is
  bought by simplifying the neighbours, not by decorating the lead: a strip of three equal cards
  made the live class as easy to miss as the one starting in two hours.
- **A card says what the role does not already know.** The page narrows a teacher's strip to the
  classes they teach, so their cards name the SECTION and drop the teacher's name; a student's rows
  are all their own section, so theirs name the teacher and badge the grade. Admins, staff,
  accountants and guardians read across sections and get both. The teacher rule keys on the
  narrowing having actually applied — a TEACHER account with no `Teacher` row sees the whole school,
  and there the name stays.
- **The second shelf is CATCH UP — the classes you missed.** It reads presence, not
  attendance: a `ConferenceParticipant` row carries `joinedAt` only once someone actually reached
  the room, and rows are created lazily at join time, so "no row with a `joinedAt`" is the signal
  and it needs no per-school opt-in. Whose presence counts is not always the reader's — a guardian
  catches up on what their CHILDREN missed. A reader who joins nothing, which is every
  administrator, sees the shelf degrade to "recently taught". The rows are one horizontally
  scrolling row of cards, up to twelve, and a card offers the recording when there is a `ready`
  one and the reader may watch it.
- **Under the shelf, two recordings ranked for the reader.** A recording of a class this reader
  MISSED outranks one of a class they sat through, and recency only breaks the tie — so two
  students in the same section are shown different pairs. Only a `ready` recording qualifies, and
  only a reader with `view_recordings` is offered the link.
- **Rename is DB-transparent.** `@@map`/`@map` keep every table / column / enum-type name.
- **Webhook + crons stay API routes** — external / scheduler-invoked; a server action can't receive them.
- **Token TTL = 5 min, client-refreshed.** LiveKit JWTs are stateless, so access revocation takes
  effect at the next refresh boundary (≤5 min), not instantly; kicks are enforced via the
  `status="removed"` gate at join.
- **Pending (non-code):** LiveKit infra provisioning (runbook ready), native OAuth credentials.
