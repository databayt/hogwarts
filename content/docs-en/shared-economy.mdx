## What you get — how the money is shared

Every month, the money left after our running costs (see [Monetize](/docs/monetize)) and the 5% community pool is shared by one rule:

1. Split it equally among everyone on the team.
2. If an equal share is more than someone's salary, that person gets their salary, no more, and steps out of the split.
3. Split what is left equally among the others, and check again. The lowest salary is met first, then the next, until the highest salary on the team is met.
4. When every salary is met, what is left is shared by score (below). This step is a proposal: the team agrees on it before the first month we reach it.

Your salary is the monthly amount written next to your name. Ask Abdout to write yours down: the rule can only pay what is written.

**Example** — four people with made-up salaries: A $300, B $500, C $800, D $1,000. The first column is the team's money that month.

| Team money | A      | B      | C      | D        |
| ---------- | ------ | ------ | ------ | -------- |
| $800       | $200   | $200   | $200   | $200     |
| $1,600     | **$300** | $433   | $433   | $433     |
| $2,200     | **$300** | **$500** | $700   | $700     |
| $2,600     | **$300** | **$500** | **$800** | **$1,000** |

**Bold** = salary met. Above $2,600, the rest is shared by score.

## Why it pays

The rule turns every school that pays into a raise for everyone, starting with whoever earns least. More paying students is the only way up, so the faster schools pay, the sooner every salary is met.

## The idea

- Anyone can read the code and improve it.
- Schools pay for the service, not for the code.
- People who build it own part of the result, not just a wage.

Two kinds of bond hold this together. Until every salary is met, we share the way a family does: equally, and whoever earns least is met first. Abdelwahab Elmessiri calls this the تراحمي bond, one of compassion. Above that, we keep a clear contract, which he calls the تعاقدي bond: every task has a rate, set before it starts, and is paid for what was delivered. Underneath both is the idea at the heart of Jamel Akbar's قص الحق: work gives a right (حق) in what it produces.

## How work is scored

Every task carries a rate before anyone starts it, so value is never argued afterwards.

- **The rate** is the base points for the kind of work × quality × impact. Code, a school signed, content, support and research each have base points.
- **Taking a task:** it is posted with its rate, in a GitHub issue or in the group. Whoever takes it writes «أنا».
- **It counts when it is accepted:** code merged, a school signed and paid, a post published, a school's problem closed.
- **Your score** is the sum of your accepted tasks, recorded under your name. It is public, and any score can be challenged with «اعتراض:».

Scores decide how money above every salary is shared (the proposal in step 4), and the shares in the first contributor agreements.

## How it works

Your work is recorded under your name: code, issues, sales, content. Sharing then starts in steps:

| When                          | What starts                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| From the first revenue        | 5% of revenue is set aside in a community pool                     |
| From any net profit           | 2.5% of net profit goes to a fund for contributors in need         |
| At $3,000 revenue a month     | The first contributor agreements: shares for the recorded work     |
| At $10,000 a month for a year | The community pool becomes a formal endowment (waqf)               |

## What it means for you today

- Do your work where it is recorded: GitHub, the CRM, the group.
- No one is paid a salary yet. The first money that comes is shared by the rule at the top, and your share grows with what you build and sell.

## When this happens

- **"What will I get?"** → the rule at the top. Equal shares until each salary is met, starting with the lowest. Your salary must be written down.
- **Your work isn't recorded** → put it where it counts today: a GitHub issue or commit, a CRM entry, or a message in the group.
- **You think a task's rate is wrong** → say so before you start it, with «اعتراض:». Once the work is accepted, the rate stands.
- **Someone asks if this is crypto or a token** → no. It is a written rule and a public record of work.

## What you do

- Keep your work under your own name, so it counts.
- Make sure your salary is written down with Abdout.
